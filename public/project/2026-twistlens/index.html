TwistLens: A Docent-Informed Image Transformation to Create Previews That Prompt Anticipation and Interpretive Experiences Before Museum Visits
ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (2026)
Summary
TwistLens is an AI-supported preview system for museum websites that transforms artwork images using docent descriptions. By preserving interpretive cues while avoiding direct visual spoilers, TwistLens supports understanding, curiosity, and anticipation before museum visits.
Citation
Thao Phuong Vu and Bokyung Lee. (2026). TwistLens: A Docent-Informed Image Transformation to Create Previews That Prompt Anticipation and Interpretive Experiences Before Museum Visits. In Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26).
Project Team
Thao Phuong Vu, Bokyung Lee
Acknowledgement
This research was supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF).
The Preview Dilemma
Museum visitors often decide whether to visit an exhibition long before entering the gallery, and this decision is largely shaped by website previews. Text descriptions provide interpretation but are difficult to visualize, while original images are easy to understand but can spoil discovery.
관람객은 전시장 방문 전에 웹사이트 프리뷰를 통해 방문 여부를 결정합니다. 텍스트는 해석 정보는 풍부하지만 시각화가 어렵고, 원본 이미지는 이해는 쉽지만 실제 관람의 발견감을 약화시킬 수 있습니다.
Twisted Preview Concept
TwistLens creates transformed previews that preserve interpretive cues while withholding spoiler-prone details. EchoLens keeps semantic meaning while changing appearance, and DecoyLens intentionally replaces selected meanings to create curiosity gaps.
TwistLens는 해석 단서는 유지하면서 스포일러 가능성이 큰 시각 요소를 변형한 프리뷰를 생성합니다. EchoLens는 의미를 유지한 채 외형을 바꾸고, DecoyLens는 일부 의미를 의도적으로 치환해 호기심을 유도합니다.
Evaluation in a Museum Scenario
In controlled evaluation, transformed previews increased anticipation, curiosity, and surprise while encouraging active interpretation compared with baseline previews.
통제 실험 결과, 변형 프리뷰는 기준 조건보다 방문 전 기대감·호기심·놀라움을 높였고, 프리뷰와 원작을 비교하는 능동적 해석을 촉진했습니다.
Original previews often over-reveal artworks, reducing anticipation before museum visits.
Twisted previews preserve interpretive cues while withholding exact visual details.
