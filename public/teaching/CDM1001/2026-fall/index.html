01 · Course overview

Learning to read culture through design

Culture shapes what people value, how they interpret experiences, and why particular products, services, and places matter. In this course, students learn to read those cultural contexts and translate research findings into coherent design-management proposals. Lectures build a shared vocabulary; workshops and critiques put it into practice.

Frame

Describe a cultural context and turn observations into a focused design opportunity.

Research

Plan and conduct field research using appropriate qualitative methods.

Synthesize

Identify patterns, tensions, and stakeholder needs across research materials.

Propose

Develop a culturally grounded design-management strategy and communicate it clearly.

02 · Semester project

From Cultural Insight to Design Strategy

Student teams select a cultural context, investigate how value is currently created and experienced, and develop a design-management proposal grounded in evidence. The project moves from field observation to synthesis, concept development, prototyping, and a final strategic narrative.

Guiding questionHow might we create meaningful value without flattening the culture we are designing with?

Core deliverables

  1. Research plan and field notes
  2. Cultural insight map
  3. Concept and strategy prototype
  4. Final proposal and presentation

05 · Working together

Course principles & policies

Participation

This is a collaborative, project-based course. Come prepared to contribute, listen closely, and give specific, constructive feedback.

Attendance

Fieldwork, workshops, and critiques are part of the learning process. Detailed attendance guidance will be confirmed in the official syllabus.

Academic integrity

Acknowledge sources, collaborators, research participants, and any AI-assisted work. Never fabricate research evidence or participant accounts.

Care in fieldwork

Seek informed consent, minimize personal data collection, and represent people and communities with accuracy and respect.
