CDM2008: Design Research

Design research turns questions about people, contexts, and experiences into evidence that can guide responsible design decisions. This course introduces a systematic process for framing research, selecting qualitative methods, conducting fieldwork, synthesizing findings, and communicating actionable insights.

Semester Focus
HUSS Future Design Program X CDM2008

Connected to the HUSS - Future Design Program, this course helps students develop interdisciplinary literacy for understanding emerging cultural, technological, and social change. Students who complete at least two courses within the program may earn the Future Design Micro-degree Certificate and become eligible to apply for scholarship opportunities.

Course Format
Flipped Learning

This course was designated as a Flipped Classroom (FC) course by the Innovation Center for Teaching & Learning (ICTL). Each week, students spend 30 minutes watching lecture videos and completing individual learning tasks provided by the instructor in advance, followed by two hours of cooperative learning with fellow students in the classroom.

Deliverable #1: [Paper Draft] Research Foundation & Initial RQ

10%
Project
Project 1
Format
Team Paper Draft
Due
2026.09.24
This deliverable is the first draft of your team’s final research paper. It should establish what your team wants to investigate, why the question matters, and how the proposed study is positioned in relation to prior research. Do not write this as a separate project proposal that will later be discarded. Continue working in the shared master manuscript and revise these sections throughout the semester. At this stage, teams are not required to define experimental variables, comparison conditions, research stimuli, measurements, or a study protocol. These elements will be developed after instructor feedback in Weeks 4–7.

Contents should include

  1. Working title & feedback questions

    • Working title and 2–3 focused questions for Week 4 feedback

  2. Introduction

    • Research context, target users or boundaries, motivation, and the unresolved design-relevant issue

  3. Related Work

    • Thematic synthesis of at least three relevant sources, their limitations, and the resulting research gap

  4. 3.1 Research Question & Hypothesis

    • One provisional RQ, expected relationship or hypothesis, and a clear scope

Presentation

No formal presentation is required.

Deliverable #2

Deliverable #2: [Presentation] Mid-term Research Proposal & Stimuli

15%
Project
Project 1
Format
Team Presentation
Due
2026.10.23
Present the research argument developed in the first three sections of the shared master manuscript. The presentation should demonstrate how the research question has been translated into comparable design conditions and testable research stimuli. The presentation is not separate from the research paper. Feedback received during the presentation should be used to revise Sections 1–3 before the team begins writing the Method section. The presentation should reflect feedback received on the Section 3 manuscript draft and research stimuli submitted before Week 7.

Contents should include

  • Research foundation: Context, key related work, research gap, refined RQ, and hypothesis or expectation
  • Comparison design: Design variable and outcomes, baseline and experimental conditions, controlled factors, confounds, and a comparison table
  • Research stimuli: Participant-facing prototype for every condition, key visuals or flows, and working links
  • Expected evidence & revision: Planned participant tasks or measures, important revisions, and remaining risks
  • A complete Method section is not required.

Presentation

  • Recommended presentation time: 10 minutes
  • Feedback and Q&A: Approximately 5 minutes
  • All team members should participate in the presentation
  • Focus on the research logic rather than visual polish alone
  • The audience should be able to trace the logic from the research gap to the RQ, conditions, and stimuli
  • Final timing may be adjusted according to enrollment and the number of teams

Deliverable #3

Deliverable #3: [Presentation] Final Design Research Study

20%
Project
Project 1
Format
Team Presentation
Due
2026.12.11
Present the completed design research study using the full research narrative developed in the shared master manuscript. The presentation should explain what the team investigated, how the study was conducted, what evidence was collected, what the evidence suggests, and how the findings contribute to design knowledge. The presentation should reflect the feedback received on the provisional Results section and during the Week 14 discussion and design-implication workshop. Feedback from this presentation should be incorporated into the Final Research Paper.

Contents should include

  1. Research foundation & design rationale

    • Context, gap, RQ or hypothesis, comparison logic, final conditions, and stimuli

  2. Method

    • Participants, study design, tasks and procedure, measures, pilot revisions, and analysis approach

  3. Results

    • Dataset overview, key quantitative comparisons, qualitative themes, and supporting figures or evidence

  4. Discussion & design implications

    • Direct RQ answer, integrated interpretation, contribution, and evidence-based implications

  5. Limitations & future work

    • Main boundaries, alternative explanations, and appropriate next steps

Presentation

  • Recommended presentation time: 12–15 minutes
  • Q&A: Approximately 5 minutes
  • All members should participate meaningfully
  • Use backup slides for detailed statistics, measurement items, and supplementary evidence
  • Clearly distinguish among:
  • results;
  • findings;
  • discussion;
  • design implications
  • The presentation should prioritize the strongest evidence and the clearest research narrative rather than attempting to show every collected data point

Deliverable #4

Deliverable #4: [Final Paper] Design Research Study

35%
Project
Project 1
Format
Team Research Paper
Due
2026.12.18
Submit the complete and revised version of the shared master manuscript developed throughout the semester. The final paper should present one coherent research argument from the research context and question to the design rationale, method, evidence, contribution, and design implications. Do not simply combine earlier homework without revision. Update terminology, RQs, hypotheses, condition descriptions, methods, figures, and claims so that every section accurately represents the final study. The paper should be understandable as an independent research document without requiring the reader to have attended the team’s presentations.

Contents should include

  1. Front matter & research foundation

    • Title, author contribution, abstract, Introduction, Related Work, and final research gap

  2. Research Question & Design Rationale

    • Final RQ or hypothesis, variables, conditions, controls, comparison rationale, and research stimuli

  3. Method

    • Participants, study design, tasks and procedure, measures, pilot revisions, analysis, and ethical data handling

  4. Results

    • Dataset overview, quantitative comparisons, qualitative themes, and clearly labeled figures or evidence

  5. 6–7. Discussion, implications & limitations

    • Direct RQ answer, integrated interpretation, contribution, bounded design implications, limitations, and future work

  6. 8–10. Conclusion, references & appendices

    • Concise conclusion, complete references, and supporting materials needed to understand or reproduce the study

No formal presentation is required.
