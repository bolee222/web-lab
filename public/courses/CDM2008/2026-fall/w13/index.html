CDM2008: Design Research

Design research turns questions about people, contexts, and experiences into evidence that can guide responsible design decisions. This course introduces a systematic process for framing research, selecting qualitative methods, conducting fieldwork, synthesizing findings, and communicating actionable insights.

Semester Focus
Future Literacy Program (Micro-degree) × CDM2008

Connected to the Future Literacy Micro-degree Certificate Program, this course gives students hands-on experience with scientific thinking as a way to identify appropriate directions for the future. Through a semester-long design research study, students will frame questions, examine people and contexts, gather and interpret evidence, and translate their findings into responsible, evidence-based design directions.

Course Format
Flipped Learning

This course was designated as a Flipped Classroom (FC) course by the Innovation Center for Teaching & Learning (ICTL). Each week, students spend 30 minutes watching lecture videos and completing individual learning tasks provided by the instructor in advance, followed by two hours of cooperative learning with fellow students in the classroom.

Nov. 27 Stage 4: Analysis

Week 13: Data Analysis #2 Quantitative Insights

Opening Questions

  • Which statistical comparison matches our study design and data?
  • What is the difference between a statistically significant result and a practically meaningful one?
  • How can qualitative evidence help explain the numerical patterns?

Learning Goal

Choose an appropriate t-test or ANOVA for the study design, interpret descriptive and inferential results, and integrate quantitative patterns with qualitative explanations.

Lecture Materials

  • FC Lecture: Data Analysis #2: Comparing Conditions with T-tests and ANOVA
  • Analysis Template
  • Paper Translation Guide · Results Writing
Unlocks when Week 12 class ends Nov. 20, 4:00 PM (KST)

In-class Activity

  • In-class Lecture:
    • Selecting a t-test or ANOVA from the study design
    • Descriptive statistics, inferential reporting, visualization, and the boundary between results and interpretation
  • In-class Activity:
    • Clean the quantitative dataset and select an appropriate analysis
    • Calculate descriptive statistics and conduct a basic t-test or ANOVA
    • Create clear figures and connect numerical patterns with qualitative themes
    • Check null, mixed, and unexpected findings for accurate reporting
  • In-class Submission:
    • Results Snapshot · one per team · before class ends
Unlocks when Week 12 class ends Nov. 20, 4:00 PM (KST)

Key Takeaways

  • Statistical analysis should follow the study design rather than be selected after seeing the results.
  • Descriptive statistics and visualizations remain necessary when inferential tests are used.
  • Statistical significance does not automatically indicate practical importance.
  • Null, mixed, or unexpected findings are legitimate research outcomes.
