CDM2008: Design Research

Design research turns questions about people, contexts, and experiences into evidence that can guide responsible design decisions. This course introduces a systematic process for framing research, selecting qualitative methods, conducting fieldwork, synthesizing findings, and communicating actionable insights.

Semester Focus
Future Literacy Program (Micro-degree) × CDM2008

Connected to the Future Literacy Micro-degree Certificate Program, this course gives students hands-on experience with scientific thinking as a way to identify appropriate directions for the future. Through a semester-long design research study, students will frame questions, examine people and contexts, gather and interpret evidence, and translate their findings into responsible, evidence-based design directions.

Course Format
Flipped Learning

This course was designated as a Flipped Classroom (FC) course by the Innovation Center for Teaching & Learning (ICTL). Each week, students spend 30 minutes watching lecture videos and completing individual learning tasks provided by the instructor in advance, followed by two hours of cooperative learning with fellow students in the classroom.

Week 11: Pilot Testing & Protocol Revision

Opening Questions

  • What kinds of failure should a useful pilot study reveal?
  • How can we distinguish participant confusion from a problem in the stimulus or protocol?
  • What must be revised before we can trust the main study?

Learning Goal

Use pilot testing to identify problems in stimuli, timing, procedure, and measurement, and revise the protocol so the main study can produce valid and reliable evidence.

Lecture Materials

  • FC Lecture: Running a Pilot Study
  • Pilot Revision Log + Final Protocol-to-Method Mapping
  • Main Study Launch Checklist
  • Paper Translation Guide · Method Finalization

In-class Activity

  • In-class Lecture:
    • Pilot issue diagnosis, revision priorities, and main-study launch criteria
    • Final Method guidance: mapping final protocol decisions into Sections 4.1–4.6
  • In-class Activity:
    • Review evidence from at least one pilot session
    • Diagnose issues by stimulus, task, protocol, measure, timing, and technology
    • Revise the protocol, conduct a final dry run, and update the final mapping
    • Complete the main-study launch check
  • In-class Submission:
    • Pilot Notes & Revision Log + final Protocol-to-Method Mapping + launch decision · one per team

  • [Study Execution Requirement] Conduct the main study and organize all qualitative and quantitative data in anonymized form. DueBefore Week 12

Key Takeaways

  • A useful pilot should reveal weaknesses rather than simply confirm that everything works.
  • Pilot problems should be traced to the stimulus, task, protocol, or measurement rather than blamed on the participant.
  • All meaningful protocol changes should be documented.
  • Main-study data should be anonymized and organized immediately.
