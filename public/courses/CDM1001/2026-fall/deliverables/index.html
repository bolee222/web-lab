CDM1001: Introduction to Culture and Design Management

Interdisciplinary thinking lies at the heart of Culture and Design Management. This course introduces fundamental perspectives, concepts, and methods across culture, design, management, UX, technology, art, branding, and marketing. Through lectures, case discussions, and interdisciplinary projects, students will learn to connect these domains and develop thoughtful strategies for real-world opportunities.

Semester Focus
HUSS Future Design Program X CDM1001

Connected to the HUSS - Future Design Program, this course helps students develop interdisciplinary literacy for understanding emerging cultural, technological, and social change. Students who complete at least two courses within the program may earn the Future Design Micro-degree Certificate and become eligible to apply for scholarship opportunities.

Industry Collaboration
Studio Mikko Laakkonen × CDM1001

This semester, students will collaborate with Studio Mikko Laakkonen, a Finnish furniture company, to explore its potential entry into the Korean market. Drawing on concepts introduced throughout the course, students will use interdisciplinary thinking to propose culturally informed strategies across design, branding, marketing, and management.

Course Format
Flipped Learning

This course was designated as a Flipped Classroom (FC) course by the Innovation Center for Teaching & Learning (ICTL). On designated flipped-learning class format, students complete lecture videos and individual preparation before class, followed by two hours of in-class application, analysis, and guided activities. Depending on the learning objective, these activities may be completed individually or collaboratively.

Deliverable #1: Pop-up Store Design Critique

18%
Project
Project 2
Format
Individual Mid-term Report
Due
2026.10.26, 11:59PM
Visit one pop-up store, marketing booth, exhibition booth, or comparable temporary branded space. Analyze how Art, Graphic Design, Product Design, Interaction Design, and Service Design shape the visitor experience, and examine how these elements work together. Go beyond description or personal preference. Use specific field observations and visual evidence to explain the design decisions, their effects, their alignment with the purpose and targeted visitors, and possible improvements.

Contents should include

  1. Case context

    • Selected space, visit context, purpose, targeted visitors, and clear distinction among official information, observation, and interpretation.

  2. Design-element analysis

    • Relevant Art, Graphic, Product/Spatial, Interaction, and Service Design decisions and their effects.

  3. Integrated critique

    • At least three meaningful relationships among design domains and their effect on the overall experience.

  4. Improvements

    • Focused recommendations grounded in field evidence and the identified relationships.

  5. Reflection

    • What the case reveals about interdisciplinary, purpose-centered, and visitor-centered design.

Presentation

No formal presentation is required.

Deliverable #2: Korean Market Entry Marketing Strategy Proposal

25%
Project
Project 3
Format
Group Presentation
Due
2026.12.08, 9:00AM
Present a clear and persuasive marketing strategy directly to Studio Mikko Laakkonen. Help the company understand the Korean market and consumers, identify the most promising opportunity, and decide how to begin approaching the market. Do not organize the presentation as a weekly process report. Use research, customer analysis, competitor comparison, and consultation only as evidence supporting the final recommendation.

Contents should include

  1. Company understanding

    • Relevant identity, design philosophy, products, and strengths.

  2. Idea and target

    • One focused design-marketing idea, target customer, and core value.

  3. Strategic rationale

    • User, customer, company, market, and competitor evidence supporting the recommendation.

  4. Development plan

    • Short- and long-term goals, actions, milestones, assumptions, and risks.

  5. Expected value

    • Benefits for the target customer, Studio Mikko Laakkonen, and its Korean-market position.

Presentation

  • Presentation time: Maximum 12 minutes per group
  • Q&A and critique: Approximately 5 minutes
  • Recommended main deck: Approximately 10–12 slides
  • Detailed research methods, full quotes, competitor tables, and intermediate analyses may be placed in the appendix.
  • All team members should participate meaningfully. Present the proposal as a professional recommendation to Studio Mikko Laakkonen.
