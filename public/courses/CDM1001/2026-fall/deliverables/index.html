CDM1001: Introduction to Culture and Design Management

Interdisciplinary thinking lies at the heart of Culture and Design Management. This course introduces fundamental perspectives, concepts, and methods across culture, design, management, UX, technology, art, branding, and marketing. Through lectures, case discussions, and interdisciplinary projects, students will learn to connect these domains and develop thoughtful strategies for real-world opportunities.

Industry Collaboration
Studio Mikko Laakkonen × CDM1001

This semester, students will collaborate with Studio Mikko Laakkonen, a Finnish furniture company, to explore its potential entry into the Korean market. Drawing on concepts introduced throughout the course, students will use interdisciplinary thinking to propose culturally informed strategies across design, branding, marketing, and management.

Course Format
Flipped Learning

This course was designated as a Flipped Classroom (FC) course by the Innovation Center for Teaching & Learning (ICTL). On designated flipped-learning class format, students complete lecture videos and individual preparation before class, followed by two hours of in-class application, analysis, and guided activities. Depending on the learning objective, these activities may be completed individually or collaboratively.

Deliverable #1

Deliverable #1: [Mini-Report] Graphic Design Self-Directed Exploration with D:Branch

9%
Project
Project 1
Format
Individual Mini-Report
Due
Sep. 29, 11:59 PM
Select one Graphic Design principle introduced in Week 4 and use D:Branch to independently explore a Graphic Design case that you genuinely want to understand. Begin with a specific personal learning goal, generate an initial branch set, examine useful and unsuccessful results, develop a follow-up question, and conduct at least one additional exploration. The purpose is not to create the most visually attractive variation. Demonstrate how you used D:Branch to investigate how a Graphic Design principle operates, how its effects may change across users or contexts, and where generated results require further verification.

Contents should include

  1. Exploration Focus

    • Selected Graphic Design principle, personal learning goal, selected case, audience, medium, context, and initial expectation.

  2. First D:Branch Exploration and Analysis

    • Complete first branch set, prompt and process documentation, comparison of design changes and effects, the most useful branch, and at least one inaccurate or unconvincing branch.

  3. Follow-up Question and Additional Exploration

    • One question arising from the first exploration and at least one additional exploration responding to it.

  4. Final Learning Synthesis

    • Main learning, conditions, exceptions, trade-offs, evidence needs, and what D:Branch did or did not help you understand.

  5. D:Branch Reflection and Self-Rating

    • Reflection on the use, limitations, accuracy, control, and possible improvements of D:Branch, plus completion of all self-rating items.

Presentation

No formal presentation is required. Selected exploration processes or findings may be briefly discussed during the following class as part of the Graphic Design review activity.

Deliverable #5

Deliverable #5: [Mid-term Report] Pop-up Store Design Critique

14%
Project
Project 2
Format
Individual Mid-term Report
Due
Oct. 26, 11:59 PM
Visit one pop-up store, marketing booth, exhibition booth, or comparable temporary branded space. Analyze how Art, Graphic Design, Product Design, Interaction Design, and Service Design shape the visitor experience, and examine how these elements work together. Go beyond description or personal preference. Use specific field observations and visual evidence to explain the design decisions, their effects, their alignment with the purpose and targeted visitors, and possible improvements.

Contents should include

  1. Case Context

    • Introduce the selected space, field-visit context, main purpose, and targeted visitors.
    • Clearly distinguish official information, direct observation, and your own interpretation.

  2. Design Elements Analysis

    • Analyze Art, Graphic Design, Product and Spatial Design, Interaction Design, and Service Design in the space.
    • For each domain, select the most relevant examples and explain the design decision, its effect on visitors, its alignment with the purpose and targeted visitors, and a justified improvement where appropriate.

  3. Integrated Design Critique

    • Examine at least three meaningful relationships among different design domains, such as support, dependency, consistency, tension, trade-off, or gap.
    • Explain how these relationships shape the overall experience and propose integrated improvements.

  4. Personal Reflection

    • Reflect on what the case reveals about interdisciplinary, purpose-centered, and visitor-centered design, and how this may influence your future design-management practice. Use photographs and other field evidence throughout the report. Place visuals near the relevant analysis and refer to them clearly in the text.

Deliverable #6

Deliverable #6: [Final Presentation] Korean Market Entry Marketing Strategy Proposal

20%
Project
Project 3
Format
Group Presentation
Due
Dec. 08, 9:00 AM
Present a clear and persuasive marketing strategy directly to Studio Mikko Laakkonen. Help the company understand the Korean market and consumers, identify the most promising opportunity, and decide how to begin approaching the market. Do not organize the presentation as a weekly process report. Use research, customer analysis, competitor comparison, and consultation only as evidence supporting the final recommendation.

Contents should include

  1. About Studio Mikko Laakkonen

    • Introduce the company’s identity, design philosophy, key products, and strengths that are most relevant to your proposal.

  2. Proposed Idea and Target Customer Persona

    • Present one new design-marketing idea, the target customer persona, and the core value the idea seeks to create.

  3. Why This Idea Is Strong

    • Explain why the proposal is convincing by combining relevant user-centered, customer-centered, and strategic thinking.
    • Use the research, customer, market, company, or competitor evidence that directly supports the idea.

  4. Short-Term and Long-Term Goals

    • Show how the idea could begin in the short term and develop over the long term, including the main actions or milestones.

  5. Expected Value

    • Explain the expected value for the target customer, Studio Mikko Laakkonen, and its position in the Korean market. Include references and team contributions in the final slide or appendix.

Presentation

  • Presentation time: Maximum 12 minutes per group
  • Q&A and critique: Approximately 5 minutes
  • Recommended main deck: Approximately 10–12 slides
  • Detailed research methods, full quotes, competitor tables, and intermediate analyses may be placed in the appendix.
  • All team members should participate meaningfully. Present the proposal as a professional recommendation to Studio Mikko Laakkonen.
